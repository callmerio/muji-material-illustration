# Japanese Kraft-Paper Instruction Style

Use this reference when generating the default visual style for the skill. It is a Japanese minimalist kraft-paper lifestyle instruction-card direction: warm, practical, quiet, and lightly nostalgic. It may be inspired by everyday Japanese product manuals and packaging inserts, but must not copy any real brand identity.

## Style Contract

Build every image as if it were printed on a high-quality recycled kraft-paper instruction card:

- Warm natural kraft-paper background close to light beige, oatmeal, and pale khaki.
- Very subtle paper fibers, fine grain, and restrained uneven tone; the sheet stays clean, flat, and cared for.
- No obvious stains, folds, tears, heavy distressing, or exaggerated vintage treatment.
- Dark brown-red or muted brick-red as the dominant single-color print; charcoal may appear sparingly for secondary text or structure.
- Low saturation, soft natural contrast, no vivid colors, neon, metallic finishes, or complex gradients.
- Simple, delicate two-dimensional hand-drawn linework. Lines are fine, even, clear, and slightly human rather than mechanically sterile.
- Objects remain structurally accurate and recognizable, with details simplified only as much as clarity allows.
- Front-facing flat composition with no photographic lens effect, real depth of field, perspective distortion, dramatic lighting, or heavy shadows.
- Generous natural whitespace. Every mark should help explain the subject or organize the page.

## Ink And Paper Palette

Use mostly one print ink per image set:

- Kraft paper: `#E6D5B8`
- Light oatmeal: `#EFE3CC`
- Pale khaki: `#D8C9A9`
- Dark brown-red: `#6E342E`
- Muted brick red: `#8B463B`
- Charcoal: `#48413A`

Prefer dark brown-red or muted brick red for diagrams, arrows, numbers, icons, and headings. Use charcoal only for a small amount of secondary text, axes, or fine structural detail. Do not introduce blue, green, yellow, purple, neon, metallic, or glossy accent colors unless the user explicitly requires factual color coding; even then, keep it subordinate to the print system.

## Drawing And Rendering

Use:

- Flat 2D line drawings with fine, consistent strokes.
- Small solid ink areas only when they improve recognition or hierarchy.
- Short dashed lines, numbered steps, simple arrows, small title boxes, and compact unbranded icons.
- Slightly imperfect print warmth, not random noise or distressed damage.
- Accurate object proportions and mechanisms, simplified for quick reading.

Do not use:

- Photorealistic photography, 3D rendering, glossy materials, thick outlines, heavy drop shadows, dramatic lighting, or fake depth.
- Exaggerated cartoon faces, childish picture-book styling, chibi characters, or decorative mascots.
- Complex gradients, volumetric light, neon, metallic ink, fake paper folds, stains, tears, or overdone retro filters.

## Layout And Typography

Organize the page like a useful life-product instruction card:

- Use a simple front-facing grid with generous outer margins and loose spacing between modules.
- Let thin rules, short dashed rules, small heading boxes, numbered steps, arrows, and consistent icons establish hierarchy.
- Keep one main title and a small number of section headings; never fill the canvas just to remove empty space.
- Use clean, upright Chinese print typography with a Japanese lifestyle magazine or product-insert feeling.
- Make the title visible but restrained. Keep body text orderly, legible, and comfortably spaced.
- Avoid calligraphy, heavy display lettering, 3D type, glow, fake embossing, and proprietary brand typefaces.
- Generate only requested words. Put long explanations outside the image in Markdown, HTML, or the surrounding layout.

## Ratios And Safe Area

Choose the ratio before prompting:

- Social card image well: `wide horizontal 1.9:1`, designed for a `936x500` image well.
- Slide/doc hero: `16:9`.
- Square post inset: `1:1`, only when the final layout needs square.

Always request:

- Full subject visible and front-facing.
- Wide safe margins, especially around Chinese labels and the title.
- No crop, no edge collisions, no crowded modules, and enough empty paper to preserve breathing room.

## Diagram And Chart Treatment

Prefer one clear explanatory structure per image:

- Cycle: numbered flat icons and a thin circular arrow.
- Pipeline: evenly spaced flat modules connected by thin arrows.
- Hub-and-spoke: a central label box with sparse, balanced branches.
- Before/after: two clean panels separated by a simple transition arrow.
- Layer stack: aligned flat layers with side labels, not perspective blocks.
- Chart: flat printed axes, bars, lines, dots, or grids with exact values and no perspective.
- Scientific mechanism: accurate line drawings and directional arrows with minimal decoration.

A quiet page is not an empty page: retain the labels and relationships needed to understand the user's idea.

## Brand Boundary

Use high-level Japanese editorial cues only. Never put MUJI or another brand name, logo, wordmark, store sign, copied packaging, proprietary typography, or recognizable branded layout into the generated image. Abstract stable visual cues into unbranded line icons instead.

## Tone Matching

- Workplace reports: practical, calm, and easy to scan.
- Education: friendly through clarity and linework, not childish decoration.
- Science and technical topics: correctness outranks atmosphere.
- Humanities: lightly nostalgic and symbolic, without inventing uncertain historical details.
- Product or daily-life explanations: useful, quiet, and close to a printed instruction insert.
